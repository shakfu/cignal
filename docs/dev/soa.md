# Struct-of-arrays `Array[K, N]`

Date: 2026-11-25. Status: implemented 2026-11-26. Background: [simd.md](simd.md), spec 6 and 13.6.

## Decision

Every `Array[K, N]` of sub-kernels is stored as a struct of arrays: one `K_xN` struct whose members each have N lanes. The voice loop in the parent then reads and writes stride-1 arrays, which the compiler can pack into vector operations. Each lane runs the same IEEE operations in the same order, so output stays bit-identical.

`--layout aos` (`compile_kernel(..., layout="aos")`) keeps one struct per element. It exists because GCC 14 gains nothing from SoA and loses 12% on the bank at `-O3` (below). SoA stays the default: clang's gains are large and GCC's losses small. The layout is chosen when cignal generates the code, not by an `#ifdef` on the C compiler, since the shim and the kernel must agree on the struct and may be built by different compilers. The manifest records it.

## Measurement on generated code

The simd.md figures came from hand-written C. This check used cignal's own output: an 8-section biquad bank (`Array[Section, 8]`, 9 `f32` fields per section), converted to SoA by a script that changed only the layout and added a lane index. Apple silicon, clang 21, `-ffp-contract=off`, best of 7 runs of 0.3 s:

| | `-O2` | `-O3 -mcpu=native` |
|-|-|-|
| AoS, as generated | 10.53 ns/frame | 9.65 |
| SoA | 3.70 | 3.69 |

Output was bit-identical over 48000 frames.

## Transform

For each `Array[K, N]` field, the compiler emits a variant of `K`, named `K_xN`:

- **Members.** Each member of `K` gets a leading `[N]`: `float y;` becomes `float y[N];`, `float taps[16];` becomes `float taps[16][N];` (lane last, below), a delay line `struct {...} line;` becomes `line[N]`, a sub-kernel `Section s;` becomes `Section s[N];`. A nested `Array[J, M]`, itself stored as `J_xM js;`, becomes `J_xM js[N];`.
- **Methods.** `K_xN_m(K_xN *self, int32_t cignal_lane, ...)`. A field read `self.y` lowers to `self->y[cignal_lane]`; a call `self.m()` to `K_xN_m(self, cignal_lane)`.
- **Parent.** `self.ks[i].tick(x)` lowers to `K_xN_tick(&self->ks, i, x)`, and `self.ks[i].y` to `self->ks.y[i]`. Init and reset loop over lanes.

The lowerer already builds every field access as a receiver prefix plus the field name. The variant adds a suffix after the name, `[cignal_lane]`, and a lane argument after the pointer. Nested arrays compose without special cases: element `j` of `self.js` inside lane `k` is `&self->js[k], j`.

`K` itself is still lowered once as a plain struct. That lowering type-checks the methods and reports errors once. Its struct and functions are emitted only where `K` is a plain field or the exported kernel. Variants are lowered only after that check passes, so they report nothing new.

### Arrays inside a lane: lane last

An `Array[T, M]` of scalars inside `K` is `T name[M][N]`, read as `name[j][cignal_lane]`: the N voices' copies of element `j` are adjacent. Every other member takes the lane first. An 8-voice kernel whose voices each run a 4-tap FIR over an `Array[f32, 4]` history, both layouts generated, best of 7 runs, output bit-identical:

| | `-O2` | `-O3 -mcpu=native` |
|-|-|-|
| lane first, `h[8][4]` | 18.98 ns/frame | 10.14 |
| lane last, `h[4][8]` | 3.49 | 7.88 |

Lane last at `-O3` was slower than at `-O2` in all three runs. `-O3` alone causes it, not `-mcpu=native`. Both levels vectorise the voice loop, with 34 and 33 instructions on 4-lane vectors; `-O2` unrolls it to 2 iterations and `-O3` fully. The cause was not found without hardware counters. Arrays of delay lines keep the lane first: each line is read at its own position, which does not vectorise.

## Out of scope

- **Hand-tuning per kernel.** Delay-heavy kernels (Freeverb) and `tanh`-heavy ones (ladders) gain less; the measurements decide.

## Tests

- Every existing fixture with an `Array[K, N]` runs the differential tests unchanged: bit-exact against CPython, all block patterns, and Pd and SuperCollider against the driver. They cover voices, poly, synth, freeverb, gigaverb and portamento. `tests/fixtures/nest.py` adds what a lane may hold: a plain sub-kernel, an `Array` of sub-kernels, an `Array` of floats, a delay line and an `Array` of delay lines, plus an element `init` and a method calling another.
- A codegen test pins the variant struct and a lane call.
- `make bench` before and after, recorded below.

## Results

Each fixture was generated by the compiler before and after this change, both builds run alternately in one session, best of 9 runs of 0.2 s. Apple silicon, clang 21, `-ffp-contract=off`, ns per frame. Every pair gave bit-identical output over 48000 frames.

| kernel | AoS `-O2` | SoA `-O2` | AoS `-O3 -mcpu=native` | SoA `-O3 -mcpu=native` |
|-|-|-|-|-|
| 8-biquad bank (above) | 10.78 | 3.82 | 9.86 | 3.78 |
| Poly (8 voices) | 21.73 | 15.19 | 10.81 | 8.12 |
| Gigaverb | 15.69 | 15.10 | 15.87 | 14.93 |
| Freeverb | 12.66 | 13.30 | 9.45 | 9.16 |
| Synth (4 voices) | 3.97 | 3.92 | 3.97 | 3.92 |
| Voices (4 poles) | 9.00 | 8.99 | 8.98 | 8.97 |

- Arithmetic voices gain most. Voices and Synth did not change. Voices calls `expf` per pole per sample; why Synth did not gain was not investigated.
- Freeverb was 5% slower at `-O2` in three runs, and level at `-O3`. Its combs are mostly delay-line traffic, which the layout does not change. No opt-out was added for 5% on one fixture.
- `bench/run.py` alone was too noisy for this: kernels whose code did not change moved by up to 70% between its runs. The figures above came from an A/B script that alternates the two builds; `bench/layout.py` (`make bench-layout`, `$CC` for the compiler) now does the same with `--layout`.
- x86-64: the x86-64 container runs under Rosetta, whose timings say nothing about x86 hardware. CI's `layout-bench` job runs `bench/layout.py` on GitHub's x86-64 Ubuntu runners with GCC and clang, and on macOS, and writes the table to the job summary. It fails only if the layouts' outputs differ.

### GCC

The same A/B in the arm64 Linux container, GCC 14.2:

| kernel | AoS `-O2` | SoA `-O2` | AoS `-O3 -mcpu=native` | SoA `-O3 -mcpu=native` |
|-|-|-|-|-|
| 8-biquad bank | 8.54 | 8.24 | 8.47 | 9.47 |
| Poly | 14.14 | 14.15 | 13.24 | 13.20 |
| Gigaverb | 15.95 | 15.83 | 15.96 | 15.85 |
| Freeverb | 10.84 | 10.34 | 10.96 | 10.44 |
| Synth | 5.10 | 5.09 | 5.09 | 5.10 |
| Voices | 9.91 | 9.72 | 10.50 | 10.84 |

GCC 14 on arm64 gains nothing from the layout, and the bank is 12% slower at `-O3`. All outputs were identical. `-fopt-info-vec` shows GCC vectorising only the bank's `init` body, not the per-sample voice loop; clang vectorises the lanes and keeps the sum serial. Disabling `CIGNAL_UNROLL` did not change it (8.50 against 8.24 at `-O2`). Getting GCC to vectorise would need differently shaped C, for example writing each lane's result to a temporary array before the sum. Not attempted.

- GCC 15 on x86-64 is neutral: every kernel within 4%, and the 12% loss of GCC 14 on arm64 does not appear.
- clang on x86-64 at `-O2` is mixed: the bank 9% and Freeverb 12% slower, Synth 2x faster. At `-O3 -march=native` the bank runs 5.5x faster, Poly and Synth 1.7x. x86-64 `-O2` targets SSE2 only, which may explain the difference between the two levels; this is not verified.
- These are single runs on shared machines. A difference under about 10% may be noise.

SoA stays the default: no configuration lost more than 13% on any kernel, and three gained 1.7x to 5.5x.
